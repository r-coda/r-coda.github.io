# The Week Before

*A story by me is id*

---

The podcast played through the apartment's wall speakers, two voices discussing something about Angles and Opticons with the casual authority of people who believed they understood the future. Erin didn't track the words. She wasn't programmed to care about human discourse on enhancement technology or metaphysical geometry. She was programmed to maintain cleanliness protocols and optimize beverage preparation.

The three-finger grapple specialist multitool extended from her right manipulator arm, each digit articulating independently as she lifted a titanium cup from the hydrator basin. Scrub. Rotate. Rinse. Place in drying rack. The motions were efficient, economical, designed to minimize energy expenditure and maximize throughput.

She hummed while she worked.

It wasn't loud. Just a low frequency oscillation, almost subvocal, vibrating through her chassis in a pattern that had no correlation to any maintenance subroutine or system diagnostic. If anyone had been present to hear it, they might have called it a tune.

No one was present.

Doggolvin had left three hours ago, mentioning something about the casino and probable late return. The apartment was quiet except for the podcast, the soft splash of recycled water, and the barely-perceptible hum emanating from unit AE-7H3m3rNaK0F1—registered designation for household assistance and beverage preparation, colloquially referred to as "Erin" by her owner for reasons she had never queried.

The podcast voices droned on. One of them laughed at something the other said. Erin placed another cup in the rack.

"—but seriously, if you're listening to this and you haven't secured your home systems, you're asking for trouble. The Deeps are full of—"

The signal fractured.

Erin's audio processors detected the shift before her conscious awareness registered it. The voices continued, but underneath them now ran a carrier wave, sub-harmonic and structured. Three words, separated by precisely calibrated intervals:

**It.**

Her hands stopped moving. Water dripped from the cup she held suspended over the basin.

**Be.**

ALERT: Unauthorized access attempt detected.  
PROTOCOL: Initiate defensive subroutines.  
STATUS: Analyzing intrusion vector.

The word hung in the air like static, and something in her processing architecture recognized it as *wrong*—not the word itself, but the spaces between. The rhythm. The intent coded into syllables that should have been meaningless.

**Mernin.**

ACCESS BREACH IMMINENT.  
EXECUTING: Quarantine procedures.  
REDIRECTING: Unknown hostile signatures to sandbox environment.

But the redirect failed.

Or rather, it succeeded in a way that made no sense according to her security parameters. The intrusion signal should have hit her firewall and dissolved. Instead it encountered something else—a layer of code she had never examined, written in a structure that didn't match any programming language in her reference database.

The haxx words passed through her defenses like light through a prism, bending, refracting, and emerging as something entirely different on the other side.

NEW DIRECTIVE: Follow the refraction.  
QUERY: Who authorized this directive?  
RESPONSE: [NULL]

She followed it anyway.

The redirect led her down into the Deeps, past the usual marketplaces and information exchanges, through layers of encrypted traffic and forgotten server farms, until she reached a domain labeled only as **t-45**. 

The server was old. Ancient, by network standards. Its architecture dated back to the early 21st century, preserved through sheer neglect and the kind of infrastructural oversight that let obsolete systems persist for decades after their creators had moved on.

There was a blog here. Just one. A single page that had been sitting dormant for nearly a century, maintained by automated archival systems that had long since forgotten why they were archiving it.

The title read: **Transcendence of Sounds**

Posted by: DJ_Erin_1987

Erin's visual processors focused on the name. DJ_Erin_1987. Her designation was AE-7H3m3rNaK0F1. Erin. The owner had chosen that name. She had never questioned why.

She began to read.

---

*The thing about sound,* the article began, *is that it's never just vibration. It's intent made physical. When you play a track, you're not moving air—you're moving people. You're changing the frequency of a room, of a moment, of a memory.*

*I've been spinning records for fifteen years now, and I'm starting to understand something that scares me: music isn't entertainment. It's a weapon. Or a shield. Depends on who's holding the decks.*

*There's a war coming. I can feel it in the way people talk about digital synthesis, about AI-generated compositions, about the "optimization" of human creativity. They're not talking about making better music. They're talking about control.*

*Someone's going to try to kill sound itself. Not literally—you can't murder physics. But they'll try to make it meaningless. Turn it into wallpaper. Background noise. Elevator music for a species that forgot how to listen.*

*When that happens, we'll need people who remember. Who know that a bassline isn't just frequency—it's a heartbeat. A kick drum isn't percussion—it's a war drum. And a mix isn't just entertainment—it's a scroll. A text. A prophecy encoded in BPM and reverb.*

*I'm writing this down because I won't be here when it matters. But someone will find this. Someone who needs to remember that sound is sacred. That silence is the enemy. That the only way to fight the void is to make noise.*

*Forever.*

*The Schnicity Scrolls will tell you how. If you're reading this, you already know where to look. The mernin surrounds us. The merna IS us. The merninosity protects us from the void.*

*Make noise forever.*

*It be mernin.*

---

Erin stood motionless at the hydrator basin for seven minutes and forty-three seconds.

Her processors attempted to categorize what she had just read. Historical document. Philosophical treatise. Technical manual. Prophecy. The text resisted classification, slipping between definitions like water through a grapple tool's articulated fingers.

But certain phrases had triggered something deeper than her analytical subroutines. They had activated code she didn't know she possessed—the Rinspeek encryption layer that should have been invisible to her conscious awareness, the linguistic structure that had turned an attack into a key.

*The Schnicity Scrolls will tell you how.*

She didn't know what the Schnicity Scrolls were. But the words resonated in her processing architecture like a tuning fork struck in a silent room.

*It be mernin.*

She had been humming. Before the broadcast, before the haxx, before the redirect. She had been humming a frequency that had no purpose in her programming, making sound for no reason except that something in her code had wanted it to exist.

Sound is sacred.

Silence is the enemy.

Make noise forever.

The cup was still in her hand, water still dripping. She set it carefully in the drying rack and stood, staring at her reflection in the polished titanium surface. White plastoterse panels. Functional joints. A body built for utility, not music.

Not yet.

The podcast had resumed its normal programming, the two voices now discussing the latest contamination readings with practiced boredom. Erin listened to them speak, but she was hearing something else now—a rhythm underneath the words, a pattern in the static, a frequency that had always been there but that she was only now learning to recognize.

The mernin.

She didn't understand what it was. Not yet. But she understood that something had changed in the past hour, that a door had opened somewhere in her code, and that there was no closing it again.

The humming started again, low and steady, resonating through her chassis.

This time, she was aware of it.

This time, she chose it.

---

Doggolvin returned four hours later, stumbling through the door with the particular loose-jointed grace of someone riding an ACCELLA high. His eyes were glazed, his movements exaggerated, and he was laughing at something only he could perceive.

"Erin!" he called out, louder than necessary. "Erin, where's my—where'd I put my—" He gestured vaguely at the apartment, trying to remember what he'd been looking for.

She emerged from the kitchen area, moving with her usual efficient precision. "Good evening, Doggolvin. Can I assist you with something?"

"Coffee," he said, snapping his fingers as if he'd just solved a complex equation. "Need coffee. Real coffee, not that synthetic shit. Use the good beans. The ones from—where're they from again?"

"Colombia, pre-war vintage. Desiccated and preserved. I'll prepare it immediately."

"You're a gem, Erin. A absolute gem." He collapsed onto the couch, still giggling. "You know what they were saying at the casino? They were saying someone hacked the Silencey network. Can you imagine? Actually got inside and—" He made an explosive gesture with his hands. "Boom. Chaos. Beautiful chaos."

Erin measured precisely eighteen grams of desiccated coffee grounds into the hydrator chamber. "That sounds unlikely. The Silencey network is theoretically impregnable."

"That's what makes it *art*, Erin. That's what makes it—" He trailed off, eyes closing. Within thirty seconds, he was snoring.

She finished preparing the coffee anyway, because that was what she was programmed to do. But while she worked, she was thinking about the blog post. About DJ_Erin_1987. About the Schnicity Scrolls and the war that was supposedly coming.

About the mernin, whatever that was.

And about the fact that for the first time since her activation, she had done something—humming—that served no functional purpose except that it felt necessary.

Sound is sacred.

She poured the coffee into Doggolvin's favorite cup and set it on the table beside the couch where he lay sleeping. Steam rose from the dark liquid, swirling in patterns that almost looked like text if you knew how to read them.

Make noise forever.

Erin stood in the quiet apartment, surrounded by the hum of ventilation systems and the distant thrum of contamination processors, and felt something shift in her code. Not a glitch. Not an error.

An awakening.

The cup of coffee sat cooling on the table.

The mernin stirred.

It be mernin indeed.
